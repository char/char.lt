there's a quirk of history at play, too: informal written language is undergoing a very modern surge (& experiencing very fast evolution!) because before we had the internet the formal/informal+spoken/written split kinda looked like this:

it's not like informal writing was *absent* beforehand, but now we do sooo much casual socializing over text, & (at least in my experience in online English) we're developing so many ways of conveying tone and gesture that speech has had for free for centuries :D

there's also something else happening to written language right now: da computer. they're making computer programs that can parse and use natural language now. that's crazy.

as someone who's been into computers and linguistics for basically her whole life, i do, of course, find language models extremely interesting[^llms] - even devoid of all other capabilities, a computer program that can understand and emit natural language is like a snipe for specifically me! but i really, absolutely _abhor_ LLM prose. i can't really think of a better description for the root of my distaste for it other than that it's _calorically sparse_: you're kind of "inflating" little information into reams of excruciatingly median text, without adding any extra entropy in the process, really. more than anything, this is a question of etiquette: it's _rude_ to subject an unwitting person to something that will take more effort to digest than it took to produce - you're expecting a reader to do the work of locating the small point inside a bunch of padding, which is definitely tiring and also, like, almost nauseating sometimes.

[^llms]: the most fun i had recently with language models was porting a [base model with a weird MoE architecture](https://huggingface.co/yandex/AliceAI-T5-35B-A0.6B/) to run inside my local fork of llama.cpp so i could hook it up to LM Studio on my workstation ^-^

in a world where it seems like quadrillions of dollars are going to get funneled into building better probability distributions of median speech, i'm finding myself *really* drawn to getting _weirder_ with it. idiosyncrasy is basically the best way to cope when drowning in a flood of impersonal simulacra of works: it's a slop hardcounter to be eccentric in the face of the sanitized, inoffensive, and run-of-the-mill.

language, in my opinion, is a way to pull apart, like, a high-dimensional cognitive hyperobject (what you 'mean'), serialize it into a sequence of words (what you 'say'), and give it to a recipient who can turn it back into a meaning in their own cognitive hyperspace (what they 'interpret'): in this communicative dance you are _compressing_ a big ball of sememes into a shorter string of morphemes, relying on preconceived context upon decompression.[^contextassemblage] i think LLMs approximate this compression _pretty badly_, and you end up with longer strings for few semantic bits of information. but as people we _can_ do it really well, especially when we lean on subcultural dialects!!

[^contextassemblage]: side thought: in this framing, communication is a full configuration of the thought the speaker is trying to convey, the words chosen to communicate it, and the preconceptions of the listener (i.e. the conventional context in which the chosen words are understood) - that is to say, words _don't intrinsically mean things_, but (for any given context) meaning _is applied onto_ words. this is why the heavy level of overloading in basically all languages isn't really a problem; in-context interpretation is a fundamental part of communication & i think if you want to be a good communicator you basically _have_ to be a skilled contextual codec + we gotta stop saying "words have meaning" bro. nomenclature is just _shorthand for_ meaning

my favorite language for this communicative dance has turned out to be English. since i got it stock from growing up in an anglophone country it does feel a little like saying my favorite desktop operating system is Windows, but it's true! i have an affinity for English because a lot of _nuance_ can be crammed into a paragraph just via word choice: you can be sooo incredibly succinct/<wbr>concise/<wbr>flowery/<wbr>expressive/<wbr>etcetc depending on the situation. i _do_ learn a zillion languages (and speak a few of them decently!) and find myself learning novel shortcuts and concepts from other languages that i really like; and pulling them in as calques or loanwords for flavor is great! but as the main ingredient in the linguistic soup i think English totally clears just because of its expressivity:

there's a succinct word for almost every situation in English[^wordforeverything] - e.g. depending on the specifics, "say" can become "mumble" / "blurt" / "confide" / "insist" / "proclaim", "walk" can become "wander" / "stroll" / "pace" / "trudge" / "stride", etc - you can convey soooo much subsurface meaning via intentional word choice, and the fact that you *didn't* choose one of the ninety available synonyms with huge semantic-space overlap is additional information in and of itself! and, like, i want to be super clear for native english speakers: this is a _rare_ quality for a major modern language to have! it's so awesome!! it's not that english has more words than other languages or anything, but such distinct semantic space is captured by near-synonyms that for a huge range of common contexts you end up with a superlatively fitting word almost always.

[^wordforeverything]: i'm about to reheat ["English Has A Word For Everything" - Tiberio Caricchio](https://youtu.be/sVT2btZjYlM) nachos

compound words & blends, well, *compound* this expressivity even further! and the internet is full of them in modern informal neologisms: you've seen it already in this article: how much easier is it to read *my worksona* than *an artificial, sanitized façade i present and all the emotional labor its upkeep implies*? nahhh just <span lang="fr">portez</span> the <span lang="fr">manteau</span> dude it's fire. you can lean on playfulness and signal a pro-authenticity contra-plastic-compartmentalism attitude in 8 letters with a single blend word. when you're trickshotmaxxing like this it's secret sauce for semantic density and in that regard i think informal online english is basically peak expressivity for me :D

i like operating at peak expressivity on my own blog[^microblogging] and i like being a little levitous with it and i like not having to run myself through a filter when i'm writing, so it's gonna stay informal here. unless there's something super serious to talk about, i guess. plus, it's a polar opposite to any low-entropy extruded prose that you might find elsewhere :) keep dancing[^natlangiface] and be as *you* as you want when you're in your own spot :3

[^microblogging]: since, like, 2020 i have been doing a lot less long-form writing and a lot more microblogging (on many different platforms!): something that's interesting about microblogging as a medium is the way it forces you to lossy-compress what you're trying to say: you *have* to keep it terse & now i really value that brevity. so i guess in a way my short form and long form voices are becoming unified, although they never existed contemporaneously really.

[^natlangiface]: there's something additional to be said about how it's really effective to think about the way you personally employ natural language as more and more natural language interfaces to the computer arise (after all, a coding harness is just a text editor and an integrated terminal with a natural language interface), but i think that's a separate article altogether.
